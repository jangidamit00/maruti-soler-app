Solar information

Things worth knowing before you install.

3 types of solar plants
  • On-grid: connected to the electricity grid, no battery. Cheapest and most common for homes. Government subsidy applies only to this type.
  • Off-grid: disconnected from the grid, works with a battery. More expensive, and no government subsidy on this.
  • Hybrid: both grid and battery. Keeps working during a power cut. No subsidy on the battery cost.
What is net metering?

During the day, any extra electricity your plant produces beyond what your home uses flows back into the grid. Your electricity company deducts this from your bill, so your bill can become very low or even zero.

How does the subsidy work? (PM Surya Ghar)
  1. Register at pmsuryaghar.gov.in with your state, electricity provider and consumer number.
  2. Get approval from your electricity provider.
  3. Get the plant installed by a government-registered installer.
  4. After the net meter is installed and inspected, a report is filed on the portal.
  5. The subsidy is paid directly into your bank account.

Subsidy: a fixed ₹78,000 for plants from 3 kW to 10 kW. No subsidy above 10 kW. Households that get 100-150 free units also get an extra ₹17,000 from the state government, making the total ₹95,000 (78,000 + 17,000). This scheme runs until March 2027.

How much will you save?

Each kW of plant produces an average of about 4.5 units a day. A 3 kW plant produces about 405 units a month. The higher your electricity rate, the faster the plant pays for itself. See the Calculator tab for the exact numbers.

Bank loan and interest
  • About 5.6% annual interest for a 3 kW plant.
  • About 7% to 7.75% for plants above 3 kW.
  • The app shows the monthly instalment for 1 to 5 year loans, on the amount left after subsidy.
Panel size

Each panel is assumed to be about 4.5 feet × 8 feet (36 sq ft); this is used to work out the total space needed for the panels.

How the bill is converted to units
  • First, the connection's fixed charge is deducted from the bill: ₹400 for a 3 kW connection, ₹500 for 5 kW, ₹800 for 6-10 kW, and ₹160 × kW above 10 kW.
  • The remaining amount is divided by a rate: ₹5.6 for a 3 kW connection, ₹10 for others (₹7.95 for a domestic 4-10 kW connection when usage works out above 500 units).
  • The resulting units are used to work out the plant size, panels, cost and everything else.
Cost and maintenance
  • Panels usually last 25 years; the inverter warranty is shorter (5 to 10 years).
  • Clean the panels with water a few times a year, especially in dusty areas.
  • Don't install panels in a shaded spot, it reduces the power generated.
What to check when choosing an installer
  • They should be government-registered (required for the subsidy).
  • The quote should list the panel and inverter brand and warranty.
  • Compare quotes from 2-3 places.
  • They should commit to service after installation too.
Kharcha aur maintenance
  • Panel aam taur par 25 saal chalte hain, inverter ki warranty kam hoti hai (5 se 10 saal).
  • Saal mein kuch baar panel ko paani se saaf karo, khaaskar dhool wale ilaake mein.
  • Chhaon wali jagah par panel na lagwao, isse bijli kam banti hai.
Installer chunte waqt kya dekhein?
  • Wo sarkar se registered ho (subsidy ke liye zaroori).
  • Quote mein panel aur inverter ka brand aur warranty likhi ho.
  • 2-3 jagah se quote lekar milao.
  • Installation ke baad bhi service dene ka vaada ho.
